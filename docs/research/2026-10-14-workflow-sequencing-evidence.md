# Workflow sequencing methods: evidence and skill recommendations

**Question.** What should Resonance borrow from Musk’s five-step engineering process, Toyota Production System (TPS), Theory of Constraints (TOC), TRIZ, and YAGNI when improving workflow guidance?

**Scope.** Research note for skill authors. It compares useful mechanisms and failure modes; it does not edit skills. Existing context includes the expanded [Necessity Protocol](../../.agents/skills/ops/core/references/necessity_protocol.md) and first-principles reference. These methods are compared here as conceptual parallels; the sources reviewed do not establish that one method historically influenced another. The recommendation is to add only distinctions those documents do not already make.

## Finding

Do not encode a universal fixed sequence, delete quota, or “automation last” rule. Use a conditional workflow: establish the outcome and safeguards, observe the real work, remove unsupported requirements and steps, resolve contradictions and constraints, improve flow and quality, then automate stable repetitive work. Measurement, quality checks, access controls, and safety interlocks may be needed at the start. Re-identify the constraint after a change.

| Method | Supported contribution | Limit for skill guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Musk’s five steps | A memorable order for questioning requirements, deleting parts/processes, simplifying, accelerating cycle time, and automating. The interview says some deleted parts or process steps will need to be added back. | A single interview gives advice, not a validated universal sequence or a statistically grounded quota. Treat the order as a prompt for a candidate design review. Do not apply speed pressure before quality and safety are understood. |
| TPS | Improve flow and lead time with Just-in-Time, while jidoka detects abnormalities and can stop work to prevent defects moving downstream. Toyota describes “automation with a human touch” and improvement of manual work before mechanization. | TPS has coupled principles and a production context. “Automate last” erases jidoka’s early detection and stop conditions. Use the quality-at-source principle where defects can propagate. |
| TOC | Identify the system constraint, exploit it, subordinate other work to it, elevate it where needed, and repeat when the constraint moves. | The current constraint may be a policy, demand, dependency, capacity, or quality issue; do not assume it is the busiest worker or longest task. TOC concerns system goal/throughput, while a project’s critical path is a dependency schedule and a queue bottleneck is a capacity/flow issue. Diagnose which applies. |
| Little’s Law | Connects the average number of items in a defined system, the flow rate, and average time in that system. Its application includes steady-state analysis and broader sample-path results. | The 1961 proof gives sufficient stationarity conditions, not a claim that stationarity is necessary for every later version. Match boundary, time window, population/cohort, and flow accounting. A growing queue or selected cohort can make naive finite-window averages misleading; the equation gives no individual completion promise. |
| TRIZ | Starts with problem formulation and ideality, with tools including Ideal Final Result (IFR), functional analysis/trimming, contradiction solving, and system evolution. | It is a broader methodology, not synonymous with the 40 principles. IFR and trimming have substantial overlap with the existing first-principles and Necessity Protocol; do not add a parallel TRIZ checklist without demonstrated lift. |
| YAGNI | Defer speculative functionality until a real need exists; it may never be needed or may take a different form. | YAGNI is about timing and speculative design, not skipping current requirements, validation, reliability, or safety work. |

## Evidence and interpretation

- **Musk’s source and the 10% wording.** The source located is Tim Dodd’s [Everyday Astronaut Starbase Tour, Part 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t705r8ICkRw), published August 2021; the discussion begins around 13:30. I did not play or inspect the video itself in this research pass. I inspected a searchable transcript mirror ([Filmot transcript](https://filmot.com/sidebyside/t705r8ICkRw/en/en/English/English/Starbase%2BTour%2Bwith%2BElon%2BMusk%2B%5BPART%2B1%2B%2BSummer%2B2021%5D)), which attributes the segment to the original video. It reports Musk saying to delete parts/process steps and that if you do not add back at least 10% of what you removed, you did not delete enough. The denominator is the set of items removed, with some needing restoration, not 10% of the entire design. This remains a personal heuristic reported via a transcript, not an empirically measured optimum or quota.
- **Toyota puts quality controls inside the work.** Toyota’s official [TPS description](https://global.toyota/en/company/vision-and-philosophy/production-system/) defines its two pillars as jidoka and Just-in-Time. Its [virtual plant tour](https://global.toyota/en/company/plant-tours/production-system/) says jidoka differs from ordinary automation and is intended to make work easier. Toyota’s Japanese official description gives concrete jidoka behavior: detect abnormalities, stop, and prevent defective output from flowing on. It also describes first improving manual work before mechanization ([Toyota TPS, Japanese](https://global.toyota/jp/company/vision-and-philosophy/production-system/)). This directly contradicts a broad reading of “automation last” that delays measurement or stop conditions.
- **TOC makes the sequence iterative.** Goldratt Japan’s [Five Focusing Steps](https://www.goldratt.co.jp/toc-dictionary/5fs) names identify, exploit, subordinate, elevate, and repeat; it says a new constraint may emerge and the cycle should continue. It also distinguishes using existing capacity from investing to elevate capacity. This is a stronger source for bottleneck-oriented work prioritization than importing Musk’s process order wholesale.
- **Do not collapse bottleneck, critical path, and queue.** A bottleneck is the system constraint limiting its goal; the critical path is a chain of dependent activities determining a project’s earliest finish; a queue forms when arrivals contend for service capacity. They may interact, but diagnosing one does not prove another. Little’s original paper, [“A Proof for the Queuing Formula: L = λW”](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.9.3.383), proves the result with finite-mean and strict-stationarity conditions. These are sufficient conditions for that theorem statement, not universal necessities: later work discusses sample-path and generalized forms ([Little, 2011 anniversary review](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.1110.0940)). For practical finite-window estimates, define the system boundary and use matched item counts and flow over a window where averages are meaningful; account for arrivals and departures and disclose a growing or shrinking backlog. Pair cycle/lead time with throughput, WIP, defect/rework, and safety measures; faster local completion can increase downstream WIP or lower quality.
- **TRIZ is broader than contradiction prompts, but mostly overlaps existing guidance here.** The [Altshuller Institute Body of Knowledge](https://www.aitriz.org/triz/triz-body-of-knowledge) lists IFR and trimming (ideal functional modeling), alongside contradiction and other tools. The institute’s [40 Principles page](https://triz.org/principles/) says those principles are one tool for technical contradictions, not the full method. IFR restates an ideal outcome independent of the current mechanism; functional analysis/trimming asks how to remove a component while preserving or transferring its useful function ([TRIZ Journal IFR tutorial](https://the-trizjournal.com/ideal-final-result-tutorial/)). These jobs are already substantially covered by the current first-principles and necessity references. Recommendation: do not add a new TRIZ workflow. Borrow the vocabulary only when a genuine function conflict is present, and validate any resulting candidate using existing checks.
- **YAGNI is compatible with necessary safeguards.** The [original XP wiki entry](https://c2.com/xp/YouArentGonnaNeedIt.html), with Kent Beck and Ron Jeffries among contributors, says to implement when needed instead of merely foreseeing future needs. It explicitly situates YAGNI among refactoring and testing practices. This supports deferring speculative structure while keeping checks that protect currently requested behavior.

## Recommendations for skill authors

1. **Make ordering conditional.** Before deleting or accelerating, establish the desired outcome, required behavior, and applicable safety, security, legal, quality, and reliability constraints. Observe actual work or inspect evidence. Then challenge requirements, remove unsupported work, simplify, and tune speed. This extends the current necessity protocol’s scope and safeguard preservation without imposing a longer checklist on routine edits.
2. **Separate instrumentation from mechanization.** Measure and validate early when the decision depends on unknown performance or failure conditions. Automate only a repeatable process whose inputs, outputs, error paths, and stop/escalation behavior are understood. Keep human or system checks at trust and safety boundaries even if local throughput improves.
3. **Aim at the system constraint, not local busyness.** State the goal and observed constraint; distinguish dependency critical path, capacity bottleneck, queue delay, and quality rework. Protect constraint time from starvation and avoid feeding it work faster than it can process. Recheck the constraint after interventions.
4. **Use deletion as a reversible hypothesis.** Name the requirement owner, evidence, affected callers/users, expected benefit, and the observable behavior that would prove the cut wrong. Trial or stage a reversible reduction where possible. Musk’s 10% remark can be cited as a personal heuristic, not a success metric.
5. **Do not add a TRIZ subroutine to routine workflows.** If the existing necessity/first-principles process surfaces a real contradiction, TRIZ’s IFR and function-trimming concepts may help generate a candidate. Reuse the existing checks to evaluate it; do not duplicate the workflow.

These additions are distinct from the existing necessity and first-principles guidance mainly in workflow sequencing, system constraint diagnosis, quality controls during automation, and the distinction between a heuristic quota and a measured target. Do not copy the whole five-step process into every skill.

## Source limits

The Musk evidence here is a secondary transcript of the located original interview; I did not inspect the video directly. No independent outcome data verifies the sequence or 10% heuristic. Toyota’s official material describes Toyota’s production system, so transfer to software or other work is a conceptual analogy whose fit must be checked. Goldratt Japan is an authoritative TOC source, but the cited summary does not validate every cross-domain application. Little’s 1961 proof gives sufficient stationarity conditions; later generalized results exist, and even a valid average relationship cannot be used as an individual service-time promise. The Altshuller Institute describes TRIZ and its tools, not their success rate in a given domain. XP wiki is a primary community record of the YAGNI practice, not a controlled comparison. These traditions are compared for useful parallels; no historical lineage between them is claimed.
