# Deal Desk and Discounting

> The deal desk is a guardrail, not a toll booth. It exists to protect margin and pricing integrity and to move complex deals through a clear path, not to slow every clean deal with an approval. Auto-approve the standard, govern the exceptions, publish the SLA.

## 1. What a Deal Desk Is For

A deal desk governs non-standard deals. Its jobs:

- **Protect margin.** Keep discounts and terms inside the bounds where the deal is still worth doing.
- **Protect pricing integrity.** Stop the slide where every rep discounts because the last one did, until list price means nothing.
- **De-risk complex deals.** Make sure non-standard terms, custom SLAs, and multi-year structures are closeable and safe before they are promised.
- **Speed the hard deals.** Give complex deals a clear, fast path to approval instead of an ad hoc scramble.

It is a commercial function, not a compliance department. The measure of a good desk is that reps route to it because it helps them close, not around it because it slows them down.

## 2. The Guardrail Rule: Auto-Approve the Standard

The design principle that separates a guardrail from bureaucracy: the desk should never touch a standard deal. Standard price, standard terms, inside the discount band, auto-approved and instant. The desk's attention is reserved for the real exceptions.

If the desk reviews every deal, two things happen. Deals slow down, and reps learn to work around the desk, which defeats the point entirely. Set the thresholds so 80 percent of deals never reach a human, and the desk can actually inspect the 20 percent that matter.

## 3. When to Engage the Desk

Escalate a deal to the desk when it crosses any exception line:

- Discount deeper than the standard band (for example, more than 20 percent off list).
- ACV above the standard threshold (for example, above 25K, set to your own number).
- Non-standard payment terms (net-90, milestone-based, quarterly).
- Multi-year contracts with custom pricing.
- Custom legal terms, SLAs, security addenda, or data processing agreements.

Everything below every line is standard and auto-approves.

## 4. Approval Tiers

Match the approver to the size of the exception, and publish a turnaround SLA for each tier:

| Deal condition | Approver | Turnaround SLA |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Standard price and terms | Auto-approved | Instant |
| Discount up to 20% | Sales manager | 24 hours |
| Discount 20% to 40% | VP Sales | 48 hours |
| Discount above 40%, or custom terms | Deal desk plus Finance | 72 hours |
| Multi-year or enterprise custom | Finance plus Legal | 5 business days |

The tiers do two jobs at once: they keep small exceptions fast and cheap to approve, and they make sure the big ones get the right eyes. The SLA column is not decoration; a tier with no published turnaround is where deals go to stall.

## 5. Discount Governance

Discounting is where margin quietly bleeds. Govern it with data, not vibes:

- **Track every discount** as both a percentage and a dollar amount, on every deal.
- **Report discount by rep, by segment, and by competitor.** Patterns tell you where the pricing or the selling is weak.
- **Require a reason** for any discount past the standard band. "To close it" is not a reason; "competitor undercut on a 3-year" is.
- **Approve the exception, not the habit.** A one-off strategic discount is fine. A rep who discounts every deal has a selling problem or a pricing problem, and more discount will not fix either.

## 6. Speed Is the Point

A deal desk that is slow is worse than no deal desk, because it adds delay to exactly the complex, high-value deals that can least afford it, and it trains your best reps to route around it. Design for speed:

- Publish the SLA for every tier and hold to it.
- Auto-approve the standard so the queue only holds real exceptions.
- Give reps a self-serve view of what is standard, so they structure deals inside the lines without asking.

The desk should feel like a fast lane for hard deals, not a checkpoint on every one.

## 7. What the Desk Reads From Discount Data

The discount data is a diagnostic on pricing and positioning. Two readings matter most:

- **If more than half of deals need a discount to close, the list price is wrong.** The market is telling you the price, and every rep is negotiating against a number the market does not accept. Fix list price (delegate the pricing work to `resonance-strategy-venture`), do not normalize the discount.
- **If everyone asks for the same exception, make it standard.** A term that every enterprise deal requires is not an exception, it is your enterprise standard that has not been written down yet. Codify it and take it off the desk's plate.

The desk is not only a gate. It is the sharpest read you have on whether your pricing matches your market.
