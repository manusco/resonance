# Comp Plan Design

> You get the behavior you pay for, measured to the decimal. The comp plan is not an HR document, it is the steering wheel of the sales floor. Decide the behavior you want first, then write the plan that produces exactly that.

## 1. The First Principle: Comp Drives Behavior

Reps optimize their commission statement with more precision than any strategy deck. This is not cynicism, it is how commission works, and it is a tool. Every plan quietly answers a question: what behavior does this reward? Get the answer wrong and you pay for the thing you say you want to stop.

- Pay only on signature, and you reward deals that churn in month two.
- Pay on top-line revenue, and you reward discounting to close volume.
- Pay on gross profit, and discounting slows on its own.
- Pay on retained or expansion revenue, and reps chase durable accounts.

So the design order is fixed: name the behavior you want, then build the plan that produces it. Never the reverse.

## 2. The Building Blocks

```
OTE (on-target earnings) = Base salary + Target variable (target incentive)
Pay mix                  = Base : Variable   (50/50 typical for AEs)
Commission rate          = Target variable / Annual quota
```

Worked example: an AE on 200,000 OTE at a 50/50 mix has 100,000 base and 100,000 target variable. On a 1,000,000 quota, the commission rate is `100,000 / 1,000,000 = 10%` of bookings.

Pay mix signals the role:

| Mix (base/variable) | Signals | Fits |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 50/50 | Aggressive, deal outcome is mostly in the rep's control | Full-cycle AEs, hunters |
| 60/40 or 70/30 | More base, longer cycles, more team selling | Enterprise AEs, complex deals |
| 70/30 or higher base | Outcome partly outside rep control | SDRs, CS, account managers |

## 3. Accelerators and Why You Never Cap

An accelerator pays a higher commission rate above 100 percent attainment. It exists to reward overperformance and to stop your best reps from coasting once they hit target.

```
Example accelerator schedule
  0% to 100% of quota      : 1.0x base rate
  100% to 125%             : 1.5x base rate
  above 125%               : 2.0x base rate
```

And the rule that goes with it: never cap commissions. A cap tells your best rep to stop selling once they hit the ceiling, and it teaches them to sandbag deals into the next period so this period does not "waste" over the cap. A capped plan punishes exactly the behavior you most want. If you are worried a rep will earn "too much," your quota is too low, which is a quota problem, not a comp problem. Fix the quota, keep the plan uncapped.

## 4. Thresholds and Decelerators

- **Threshold (commission floor):** commission starts only above a floor, say 40 to 50 percent of quota. It guards against paying meaningful commission for near-zero production. Use it with care; set the floor too high and you demoralize a rep having a slow but recoverable quarter.
- **Decelerator:** a lower rate below the threshold. Rarely worth the complexity. Most plans use a clean threshold instead.

Thresholds are a guardrail, not a punishment. The default is no threshold or a low one; add a higher floor only when you have a real problem with reps coasting on a base salary.

## 5. Clawbacks: Paying for Durable Revenue

A clawback recovers paid commission when a deal does not turn into durable revenue: it churns, refunds, or fails to pay within a defined window (commonly 90 to 180 days).

Use a clawback when the business can be hurt by a signature that does not stick:

- Fast early churn (deals cancel in the first few months).
- Non-payment or failed collection.
- Sandbagging quality to hit a number, knowing the account will not last.

The clawback aligns the rep to revenue that is collected and retained, not to the signature alone. Pair it, where you can, with paying commission on collected or retained revenue rather than on bookings, so the incentive points at durable dollars from the start. Announce clawbacks clearly and apply them consistently; a surprise clawback destroys trust faster than no clawback at all.

## 6. Aligning the Metric to the Motion

Pay on the one thing you want more of, per role:

| Role / motion | Pay primarily on | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| New-business AE | New ARR / new logos | You want net-new growth |
| Account manager / CS | Net revenue retention, expansion | You want the base to grow and stay |
| Deals that churn fast | Retained or collected revenue, plus clawback | You want durable revenue, not signatures |
| Margin under pressure from discounting | Gross profit, not top-line | You want profitable deals, not cheap ones |
| Strategic short-term push | A time-boxed SPIF on the specific behavior | You want a burst, then back to the core plan |

SPIFs (short-term incentives) are a scalpel, not a staple. Use them for a specific, time-boxed push, and retire them. A floor of standing SPIFs turns into a plan reps game instead of a motion they run.

## 7. The Simplicity Test

A rep must be able to compute their own commission on a napkin. If they cannot, the plan is not steering behavior, it is confusing it, because a rep cannot optimize for an incentive they cannot calculate.

- One primary metric per role. Secondary metrics dilute focus.
- A rep should always know what the next deal is worth to them personally.
- If explaining the plan takes more than a few minutes, cut something.

Complexity is not sophistication in a comp plan. It is friction between the rep and the behavior you are paying for.

## 8. Anti-Patterns

| Anti-pattern | What it produces | Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Capped commissions | Sandbagging, top reps coast or leave | Uncap; fix the quota if payouts scare you |
| Pay on signature with a churn problem | Deals that cancel in month two | Clawback and pay on retained revenue |
| Pay on top-line with a margin problem | Discounting to close volume | Pay on gross profit |
| Too many metrics | Diluted focus, reps confused | One primary metric per role |
| Standing SPIFs everywhere | Reps game the SPIFs, ignore the core motion | Time-box SPIFs, retire them |
| Changing the plan mid-year | Lost trust, reps stop believing the number | Set it annually, hold it, change only with cause |
