# First Principles Protocol

Use this optional method when a material decision rests on an inherited or uncertain premise. Routine reversible work needs no extra research.

## Work from the actual constraints

1. State the requested outcome, explicit constraints, and safeguards. Derive the conditions needed for that outcome, separating necessary functions from inherited mechanisms. Do not turn a proposed solution into the goal or widen the request while decomposing it.
2. List only premises that could change the choice. Record source, owner, and confidence. Premises may be logical/mathematical, physical/technical, empirical, legal/contractual, security-related, conventional/interface-based, or explicit preferences. Each needs evidence suited to its kind; none is automatically disposable or absolute.
3. Challenge each premise in the right way: derive or find a counterexample, measure in the relevant environment, check a governing source and its applicability, inspect callers and consumers, or ask the responsible owner. Use another source when stakes, uncertainty, or contradiction warrant it. A proof inside a model does not establish that its assumptions fit the real conditions. Mark what remains unknown.
4. Reconstruct a candidate from the conditions that survive, including integration and operating cost. Before accepting it, check fit to those conditions and relevant domain, boundary, and failure requirements. Compare it with the inherited approach and other viable alternatives. Before removing a convention, find the compatibility, coordination, or user job it may serve and account for migration and reversal cost. An analogy is a lead, not proof. Reuse it when conditions still match; check what differs.
5. Stop when another layer of decomposition cannot change the decision or protect a requirement. State a test or observation suited to the claim, what result would overturn the choice, and the limit of what it proves. Do not invent a measure when the question has no meaningful measurable outcome.

## Worked examples

A three-day release is not a three-day test. Measure code checks separately from review, QA, security or legal approval, rollout, and recovery. Five-minute CI may improve one stage; it does not prove that the full release can safely take five minutes. Confirm each gate's purpose and owner before changing it.

Raw-material cost is not finished-battery cost. Include processing and yield, equipment and labor, quality, integration, logistics, safety, warranty, and failure obligations before comparing delivered systems. A lower input price alone does not prove lower production cost.

Check legal, contractual, security, and team conventions for source, applicability, owner, and current job. Keep applicable obligations and safeguards. Change a convention when evidence supports a better way to meet the outcome while preserving its necessary job, compatibility, and behavior; remove it when that job is no longer needed. This method exposes assumptions; it does not excuse ignoring them.
