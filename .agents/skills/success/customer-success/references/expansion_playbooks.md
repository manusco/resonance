# Expansion Playbooks

> Expansion is the cheapest revenue in the business and the engine that pushes NRR past 100%. The rule is sequence and signal: expand only after value is proven, and trigger the motion off a real usage signal, never off a quota clock.

## 1. Why expansion is the cheapest growth

A dollar of expansion revenue costs a fraction of a dollar of new-logo revenue. There is no acquisition spend, trust is already earned, the product is already deployed, and the sales cycle is short because the buyer has felt the value. It also carries the highest margin and the shortest path to cash.

This is why NRR above 100% is the strongest signal in SaaS: the existing base grows on its own, and expansion outruns churn. A business with 120% NRR can slow new sales to a trickle and still grow. Expansion is where that number is built.

## 2. Expand after value, never before

Sequence is everything. Pushing more product at a customer who has not yet reached first value poisons both the current subscription and the expansion. The order is fixed:

1. First value reached.
2. Habit and breadth established.
3. Outcome proven and visible to the buyer.
4. Then, and only then, expand.

An expansion sold before value is a churn with a bigger number attached. Prove the current purchase first; the expansion follows the proof.

## 3. Types of expansion

| Type | What grows | Natural trigger |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| **Seats** | More users on the same product | Team growing, active seats near the licensed count |
| **Usage or tier** | Higher volume or a higher plan | Usage approaching a cap, or a limit hit repeatedly |
| **Cross-sell** | An adjacent product or module | A new use case surfacing in a QBR or ticket |
| **Upsell** | Premium features, support, or SLA | An enterprise need appearing (security, control, scale) |

Match the play to the signal. Someone bumping a usage cap wants a tier conversation, not a cross-sell pitch for a module they have no need for.

## 4. Trigger off signals, not the calendar

The best expansion is a well-timed answer to a need the customer is already feeling. Watch for the signal and move on it.

| Signal | Expansion it points to |
|--------|------------------------|
| Active seats near licensed seats | More seats |
| Usage nearing a plan limit | Tier upgrade |
| A new team or department invited in | Seats plus a possible new use case |
| A feature request that maps to a higher tier | Upsell |
| A workflow adopted that a module extends | Cross-sell |

An expansion timed to a signal feels like help. The same expansion timed to your quarter-end feels like a squeeze and trains the customer to distrust the outreach.

## 5. The QBR as an expansion moment

A quarterly business review is not a status meeting. It is where you prove the outcome to the buyer and set up the next step. A QBR that recites usage stats and ends is a wasted hour.

Run it as: outcome delivered (tie usage to their business result, in their numbers), then plan for the next quarter (the next outcome and what it needs), then the natural expansion that the next outcome requires. Expansion proposed inside a proven-value conversation lands as a logical next step, not a pitch. The QBR is also the highest-signal voice-of-customer session you get: capture the blockers and requests and route them to product.

## 6. Who runs the expansion

- On smaller accounts, the CSM owns the whole motion, including the expansion, because splitting it adds cost the deal size cannot carry.
- On larger accounts, the CSM surfaces and qualifies the opportunity and an account manager or sales rep runs the commercial close. The CSM must stay in the room: they hold the relationship and the outcome story that makes the expansion credible.
- Guard the trust. The CSM's value is being the customer's advocate. If every conversation becomes a sales push, the advocacy is gone and so is the early signal. Keep the outcome work and the commercial ask distinct.

## 7. Anti-patterns

- **Expanding before value.** The largest and most common mistake: selling more to an account that has not yet succeeded with what it has.
- **Calendar-driven pushes.** Expansion outreach timed to the vendor's quarter instead of the customer's need.
- **The status-report QBR.** A meeting that recounts activity, proves no outcome, and opens no next step.
- **The CSM turned closer.** Collapsing advocacy into a permanent sales role, which kills the trust that made the early signal possible.

## 8. Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Why it matters |
|--------|------------|----------------|
| Net revenue retention | Existing-cohort revenue including expansion, over churn | The headline; above 100% means the base compounds |
| Expansion MRR | New recurring revenue from existing accounts | The raw output of the motion |
| Net new from base | Expansion minus contraction and churn, base only | The honest view of whether the base grows |
| Expansion win timing | Share of expansions tied to a usage signal vs a calendar push | Signal-timed expansion holds; squeezed expansion churns |

NRR is the number this whole skill exists to move. Read it over a fixed cohort of existing customers with no new logos mixed in, or it flatters you and teaches nothing.
