# Renewal Motion

> The renewal is a lagging outcome of everything before it. You do not create the result in the renewal quarter, you collect it. Start early, tier by risk, and forecast on evidence, because a renewal you first look at 30 days out is a renewal you have already half-lost.

## 1. The renewal is a lagging outcome

Whether an account renews was mostly decided before the renewal conversation: did it reach value, did it build a habit, is the champion still there, is the outcome visible to the buyer. The renewal motion does not manufacture those answers late. It surfaces them early enough to fix what is fixable.

So the point of an early, tiered motion is not to sell harder at the end. It is to find the risk while there is still time to act on it.

## 2. Start early, tier by risk

Not every renewal deserves the same runway. Tier by ARR and by health, and start the clock accordingly.

| Tier | Profile | Start before renewal |
|------|---------|----------------------|
| **High-touch** | Large ARR, or any account scored yellow or red | 120 to 90 days |
| **Standard** | Mid ARR, green health | 90 to 60 days |
| **Tech-touch** | Small ARR, healthy, auto-renew | 60 to 30 days, mostly automated |

The high-touch window is long on purpose: a red account 90 days out can still be saved, the same account 20 days out usually cannot. Risk buys runway.

## 3. The evidence-based forecast

A renewal forecast is a claim about the future that must rest on evidence, not on the CSM's mood. Use commit categories tied to proof.

| Forecast | Evidence required |
|----------|-------------------|
| **Commit** | Green health, outcome proven, budget confirmed, champion engaged |
| **Likely** | Healthy usage but one open item (budget, a stakeholder, a late issue) |
| **At risk** | Yellow or red health, an unresolved blocker, or a quiet account |
| **Lost** | Cancel signaled, champion gone with no replacement, budget cut |

The discipline: no green forecast on a red account. If you cannot point to the outcome evidence, the account is not a commit no matter how friendly the last call felt. Optimism is not evidence.

## 4. Multi-threading and champion risk

The most common way a healthy-looking account dies is that the single champion who ran it leaves. One relationship is a single point of failure.

- Map the account: champion, economic buyer, day-to-day users, executive sponsor. A renewal that depends on one name is at risk regardless of usage.
- Multi-thread deliberately: get the outcome visible to the economic buyer and build a second relationship before you need it.
- When a champion leaves, treat it as an immediate risk event: re-introduce value to their replacement fast, because the replacement inherited your tool without choosing it.

## 5. Auto-renew vs active renewal

- **Auto-renew** reduces passive churn on healthy small accounts by removing a manual step, and it is the right default for tech-touch. It does not remove the need to confirm value; a silent auto-renew on an unhappy account produces a chargeback or an angry non-renewal next cycle.
- **Active renewal** is for accounts where the outcome and the price both deserve a conversation. Use it for high-touch and for any expansion or increase.
- Never use auto-renew as a trap. Hiding the cancel path or burying the renewal terms buys one cycle and costs the relationship. Keep the exit honest, the same standard as any save.

## 6. Price increases at renewal

A renewal is the natural moment to align price with value delivered, done straight.

- Earn it first. A price increase lands when the outcome is proven and visible. On a struggling account it triggers the churn.
- Give notice and a reason. Tie the increase to added value or expanded usage, not to a spreadsheet target, and give the buyer time to plan the budget.
- Offer a path. A multi-year lock or an annual prepay in exchange for a softer increase pulls cash forward and secures the logo.

## 7. Anti-patterns

- **The surprise renewal.** First contact 30 days out on an account no one managed. Everything that follows is damage control.
- **Happy ears.** Forecasting commit because the last call was pleasant, with no outcome evidence behind it.
- **Single-thread complacency.** A green account resting on one champion, with no second relationship, treated as safe.
- **The silent auto-renew.** Auto-renewing an unhappy account to bank the number, then eating the chargeback and the reputation hit.

## 8. Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Target shape |
|--------|------------|--------------|
| Gross revenue retention | Retained revenue before expansion, over a cohort | As close to 100% as the segment allows |
| Logo retention | Share of accounts retained, count not dollars | Watch alongside GRR; small logos churn quietly |
| On-time renewal rate | Renewals closed by the renewal date | Late renewals often become lost ones |
| Forecast accuracy | Forecast vs actual by category | A forecast that misses is a motion that started late |

GRR is the floor. It can never exceed 100%, it measures only the leak, and it is the honest base that expansion builds on. Read it before any NRR number that includes expansion.
