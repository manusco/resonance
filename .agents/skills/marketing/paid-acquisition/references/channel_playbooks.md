# Channel Playbooks: Search vs Paid Social vs Demand Gen

> When each channel wins, and how to structure the account inside it. Platform-agnostic. Google, Meta, LinkedIn, TikTok are named as examples, not law.

## The one question that picks the channel

Is the demand already there, or must you create it?

- Someone is typing your category into a search box right now: capture it. Search wins.
- No one is looking, but the right person would care if interrupted well: create it. Paid social and demand gen win.
- The buyer is defined by job title and company, not by a search: target the role. B2B paid wins.

You cannot capture demand that does not exist. You cannot cheaply create demand for a product people already search for by name. Match the channel to the buyer's actual state.

## Search (intent capture)

The visitor announced intent by typing the query. High conversion, high cost per click, limited by search volume. You harvest, you do not manufacture.

- **Brand terms**: people searching your name. Cheapest conversions in the account. Defend them unless a competitor is not bidding and organic already owns the top spot.
- **Non-brand terms**: people searching the category or problem. The real growth lever, more expensive, needs tight query-to-landing-page match.
- **Shopping / product listing**: for e-commerce, image plus price at the moment of intent. Feed quality is the lever.
- Best for bottom of funnel. A person searching "invoice automation software" is closer to buying than a person scrolling a feed.

## Paid social and demand gen (intent creation)

The visitor was not looking. You interrupt with a message that makes them care. Massive scale, lower cost per click, the algorithm finds the audience once the creative signals who it is for.

- Meta, TikTok, YouTube, display, discovery. Visual-first. The creative does the targeting work.
- Best for top of funnel, demand creation, and products people do not yet know they need.
- Weak for capturing existing high intent. If someone already wants your named product, do not pay to interrupt them on a feed, catch them on search.

## B2B by role and account

The buyer is a role at a company, not a search query. Target by job title, seniority, industry, company size, or a named account list.

- LinkedIn and account-based paid. Very high cost per click, only justified when deal value is high (enterprise, high-ticket).
- Do not run LinkedIn CPCs against a low-ticket self-serve product; the math rarely clears.
- Pair with retargeting: paid social warms the account, search and retargeting close it.

## Retargeting (across all channels)

Show ads to people who already engaged (site visitors, video viewers, cart abandoners, engaged social audiences).

- Cheapest incremental conversions, but easy to fool yourself: some of those people would have converted anyway. Do not over-credit it.
- Exclude existing customers. Sync the paid-users list to the ad account and suppress it. Paying to advertise to people who already bought is pure waste.
- Cap frequency. Past a few impressions per day you are annoying, not persuading.

## Structure patterns per channel

Structure to feed the algorithm and to isolate only what you must control separately (budget, bid, message). Fewer, better-fed campaigns beat a sprawl of starved ad sets.

- **Search**: split brand vs non-brand into separate campaigns (different intent, different value, different bids). Group tight themes so ad copy matches the query. Keep budgets from cannibalizing.
- **Paid social**: consolidate audiences into broad targeting and let the algorithm find pockets. Separate campaigns by funnel stage (prospecting, retargeting), not by a dozen micro-interests. Over-segmentation starves each ad set below the conversion volume it needs to exit the learning phase.
- **Demand gen / video**: separate by objective and format. Feed each campaign enough budget to learn.
- **B2B / account-based**: segment by account tier or persona when the message must differ; otherwise consolidate.

The failure mode everywhere: too many tiny campaigns, each below the data volume needed to optimize. Consolidate first, segment only when a segment genuinely needs a different budget, bid, or message.

## Channel selection checklist

- [ ] Named the buyer's demand state: capturing existing intent or creating new intent?
- [ ] Picked the channel from the buyer's state, not from platform fashion.
- [ ] Confirmed the channel can clear the CAC ceiling (see paid_unit_economics.md) before committing budget.
- [ ] Structured campaigns to feed the algorithm, not to look tidy.
- [ ] Excluded existing customers from prospecting and retargeting.
