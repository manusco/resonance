## Search engines and where DACH differs

Google dominates DACH (roughly 90%+), so google.de, google.at, and google.ch are the primary targets. But non-Google share is meaningfully higher than in the US: Bing and, notably, Ecosia (a German-born engine) hold real usage. Do not ignore Bing indexing and Ecosia, and keep Bing Webmaster Tools configured, not just Search Console.

## German keyword reality: Komposita and umlauts

- **Compound words (Komposita).** German fuses concepts into single words: "Krankenversicherung", "Projektmanagementsoftware". Keyword research must capture both the compound and its split forms, because users and Google handle both. Target the compound where volume is real, and cover variants in content.
- **Umlauts and ss.** Users search with and without umlauts ("Buero" and "Buro" and "Buero"). Handle ae/oe/ue and the sharp-s vs ss variants in URLs, titles, and on-page text so you match either spelling. Swiss German drops the sharp s entirely.
- **Query length and formality.** German queries are often longer and more precise. Match the specificity; thin, broad pages lose to thorough ones.

## Sie, Du, and search intent

Address matters for both conversion and relevance. B2B and YMYL German content leans Sie; consumer and community content increasingly uses Du. Match the register your audience uses in their own queries and content, and stay consistent across the page and the site.

## Three-country targeting with hreflang

Germany, Austria, and Switzerland share a language but are distinct markets. Serving one page to all three causes duplicate-content dilution and mismatched locality.

- Use hreflang correctly: de-DE, de-AT, de-CH, and a de or x-default fallback. Switzerland also needs fr-CH and it-CH if you serve those regions.
- Localize the substance, not just the top banner: currency (EUR vs CHF), examples, legal references, phone formats, and payment norms.
- Prefer country signals (ccTLD like .at or .ch, or clear subfolders with hreflang) so each market ranks on its own engine.

## E-E-A-T the German way: Impressum and depth

- **Impressum.** A complete legal-notice page is legally required and is a strong trust and legitimacy signal. Its absence undermines both compliance and perceived authority. Author and company transparency matter here more than in casual-English niches.
- **Depth over brevity.** German users expect thorough, complete content. A short "listicle" that would rank in English often loses to the more exhaustive German page. Cover the topic fully, cite sources, and show expertise.
- **DSGVO.** Any data collection, forms, or tracking must be DSGVO-compliant. This intersects with technical SEO (consent-gated scripts, cookieless analytics options).

## Language quality is a ranking and trust factor

Grammatically wrong or machine-translated German is punished harder than sloppy English, by both users and by quality signals. Correct grammar, natural phrasing, and the right regional variant are non-negotiable. Never ship raw machine translation; edit to native quality. Apply the German anti-slop rules so the copy reads human, not like translated marketing.

## GEO for German answer engines

Generative engines answer in German too. To be cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI answers in German: write clear, factual, well-structured German, use precise headings that match German question phrasing ("Was kostet ...", "Wie funktioniert ..."), and keep entities and claims verifiable. The citability rules of GEO apply; the language and phrasing must be native German.

## The DACH SEO checklist

- [ ] Keyword research covers Komposita and their split forms, plus umlaut and ss variants.
- [ ] hreflang set for de-DE, de-AT, de-CH (plus fr-CH, it-CH where relevant) with a sensible fallback.
- [ ] Each market localized in substance: currency, legal, examples, payment, phone formats.
- [ ] Impressum and Datenschutz present; content shows depth, sources, and author transparency.
- [ ] German is native-quality, correct register (Sie or Du), consistent across the page.
- [ ] Bing and Ecosia indexing verified, not only Google.
- [ ] German question-phrased headings for answer-engine citability.
