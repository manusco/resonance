# Organic Distribution

Use this when unpaid content must reach an audience.

## First-Principles Frame

An organic post earns attention by giving the audience a useful change. The surface is only the delivery path.

Write this before any calendar:

- Audience: who is this for?
- Situation: what are they trying to do now?
- Promise: what will they understand, decide, or do after this?
- Proof: what can we show without inventing evidence?
- Channel reason: why this surface, not another?
- Next decision: what will we change if the signal is good, weak, or unclear?

## Surface Fit

- LinkedIn: professional identity, career stakes, operating lessons, clear points of view, useful field notes.
- X: fast idea testing, sharp claims, threads, live commentary, dense observations.
- Instagram: visual identity, behind-the-scenes, product context, founder or brand presence, short clips.
- TikTok and Reels: native motion, curiosity, visible transformation, fast context, clear payoff.
- YouTube: search, discovery, trust, depth, repeatable packaging, proof over time.
- Community channels: contribution, specificity, earned trust, rule awareness.
- Editorial newsletter: useful recurring point of view. Lifecycle owns it only when it is tied to activation, retention, win-back, or product education.

Verify current platform specs when exact limits, safe zones, music rights, link treatment, or feature behavior matter.

## Repurposing Tree

Start with a source asset: customer call, product lesson, research note, launch, webinar, founder point of view, or data read.

Then split it into atoms:

- Claim: the main point.
- Proof: data, quote, screenshot, result, example, before/after.
- Story: the change, error, or field lesson.
- How-to: the steps.
- Objection: what a smart skeptic would ask.
- Visual: chart, thumbnail, screen, image, clip, diagram.

Map each atom to one surface. Do not force every atom everywhere.

## Platform Adaptation

For each surface, change:

- Opening: the reason to care now.
- Evidence: the proof that fits the surface.
- Pace: short, threaded, visual, or long-form.
- CTA: comment, save, reply, subscribe, visit, or no CTA.
- Asset need: none, image, carousel, clip, thumbnail, chart, or screen.
- Risk: rights, privacy, accessibility, community rules, brand safety.

Keep the core claim stable. If adapting the format changes the claim, rewrite the claim or cut the surface.

## Community Rules

Join with value, not extraction.

- Read the rules before posting.
- Contribute before asking.
- Do not disguise promotion as help.
- Do not use engagement pods, scripted comments, or fake controversy.
- Do not paste the same post into several communities.
- Name the affiliation when it matters.

## Learning Loop

Organic content rarely proves causality by itself. Use leading signals to decide what to do next:

- Save rate or bookmark intent: useful enough to keep.
- Comment quality: problem resonance, objections, language.
- Profile or site visits: curiosity.
- Clicks: active interest, but context matters.
- Watch retention: promise match and pacing.
- Replies or DMs: high-intent conversation.

Hand any causal claim, attribution model, or experiment design to `resonance-marketing-analytics`.
