# Video Packaging

Use this for YouTube videos, Shorts, Reels, TikToks, webinar cutdowns, and clips where discovery depends on packaging.

## Package Before Script

When discovery is the job, define the package before writing the full script:

- Viewer: who is this for?
- Promise: what useful change will the viewer get?
- Tension: why does this matter now?
- Proof: what makes the promise believable?
- Surface: YouTube, Short, Reel, TikTok, webinar cutdown, or community clip.
- Opening beat: the first useful moment, paced for the surface.
- Title or caption angles: 3 to 5 options with different promises.
- Thumbnail or cover brief: visual contrast, subject, expression or object, safe zone, and text policy.
- Retention risks: where viewers will leave and what must happen before that point.

Do not use fixed timing rules as law. A TikTok, a Short, and a 25-minute YouTube explainer need different openings.

## Title And Promise

A title is a promise, not a label. It must name the useful change or tension.

Weak: "Productivity Tips"

Better: "Why Your Team Ships Less After Hiring More"

The better title names a conflict and a reason to watch.

## Thumbnail Or Cover Brief

The visual should make the promise easier to understand.

Include:

- Main subject.
- Contrast or tension.
- One focal point.
- Safe zone for text and platform UI.
- Accessibility: readable contrast, no tiny text, no essential meaning carried by color alone.
- Rights and consent: only use people, logos, screenshots, and footage that are allowed.

Do not ask an image model to render final copy when legibility matters. Hand final layout and text placement to the right production step.

## Opening Beat

The opening beat must pay off the title fast enough for the surface. It can be:

- A direct claim.
- A contradiction.
- A visual result.
- A mistake.
- A concrete before/after.
- A question the viewer already has.

Cut preamble. Do not open with generic greetings unless the format truly needs it.

## Handoffs

- Copywriter writes the final title, caption, hook, and script language.
- Studio creates thumbnail, cover, still, or generated visual assets.
- Analytics defines retention reads, click-through checks, and learning claims.
