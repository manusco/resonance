# Offer Creation Protocol

An offer is the buyer's whole decision, not only the product, price, or page.
It names a valuable outcome, proves the product can deliver it, lowers the
buyer's real risk, and stays profitable to fulfill.

Use this protocol when conversion work stalls, a campaign needs an offer, or a
price change is on the table.

## 1. Define The Buying Job

Start with the buyer's actual situation.

- **Buyer**: Who has the pain, budget, authority, and urgency?
- **Job**: What result are they trying to get or avoid?
- **Current alternative**: What do they do now if they do not buy?
- **Switching cost**: What time, money, trust, data, process, or reputation risk
  makes the change hard?
- **Buying moment**: What event makes the problem impossible to ignore now?

If the current alternative is cheaper, safer, or familiar, the offer must beat
that alternative, not a blank page.

## 2. Build The Value Case

Pressure-test the value before writing the page.

| Lever | Question | Good answer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Outcome | What concrete result changes for the buyer? | A named business or life result, not a feature list |
| Likelihood | Why should this buyer believe they can get it? | Proof, fit criteria, delivery process, or a clear mechanism |
| Speed | How quickly can the first useful result arrive? | A first milestone, not a vague promise |
| Effort | What work does the buyer avoid or get help with? | Setup, templates, service, migration, training, or defaults |
| Risk | What could make them regret buying? | A stated objection with a real answer |

Each lever needs a truthful claim. Delete or weaken claims the product cannot
prove.

## 3. Inventory Proof Before Claims

List the evidence before drafting the offer.

- Customer results with names, dates, segments, and source links.
- Product behavior the team can demonstrate live.
- Benchmarks, audits, or usage data with method and date.
- Case studies that show the before state, intervention, and after state.
- Limits and fit rules: who should not buy, or when the offer will not work.

Never use anonymous praise, invented metrics, or borrowed credibility as proof.
If proof is weak, make a smaller claim or make proof collection the next task.

## 4. Stack Value Without Adding Debt

Only add components that increase buyer confidence or reduce buyer work.

Good offer components usually do one of four jobs:

- Make the first result easier to reach.
- Remove setup or migration pain.
- Answer a common objection before sales has to.
- Help the buyer use the product correctly.

Reject filler bonuses. A component that creates delivery load without changing
the buying decision is margin leakage.

## 5. Set Price Against Value And Margin

Price is a business decision, not a copy trick.

Check:

- The buyer's current cost of doing nothing.
- The measurable value created or protected.
- Gross margin after support, onboarding, services, discounts, refunds, and
  payment costs.
- Sales cycle and acquisition cost.
- Payback period the buyer can believe.
- Comparable alternatives, including spreadsheets, agencies, internal labor, and
  delaying the decision.

If the price goes up, name what changed: proof, scope, cost, positioning,
capacity, or buyer segment. Do not hide a price change inside urgency copy.

## 6. Reverse Risk Only When It Is Fundable

Risk reversal can be a guarantee, trial, pilot, cancellation term, migration
help, proof milestone, or clear exit path. Choose the smallest promise that
answers the buyer's real fear.

Before offering it, verify:

- The team can honor it operationally.
- The refund or service cost will not break margin.
- The terms are plain and visible.
- The promise does not reward misuse or attract bad-fit buyers.
- Legal, compliance, or finance review is complete when needed.

Fake urgency, fake scarcity, and impossible guarantees are trust debt.

## 7. Protect Existing Customers In Price Changes

For active customers, a price change is a retention event.

Decide and state:

- Who is affected: new customers, renewing customers, all customers, or a named
  segment.
- What changes in value, service, limits, or cost structure.
- Whether existing customers are grandfathered, phased in, or given a choice.
- The notice period and the support path for objections.
- The cancellation or downgrade path.

The message should make the business reason clear without punishing loyalty.

## 8. Hand Off To Experimentation

When the offer is coherent, hand it to Analytics for measurement.

Write the test as:

> If we change [offer element], then [buyer action] will improve because [belief
> or risk changed].

Measure the offer decision, not only the page click. Track qualified leads,
demo show rate, activation, paid conversion, refund rate, churn, support load,
and margin when relevant.

## Failure Conditions

- The page asks for a higher conversion rate before the offer beats the current
  alternative.
- The offer depends on unsupported proof or invented urgency.
- The stack adds delivery cost without increasing buyer confidence.
- The price is justified only by competitor anchoring, not value and margin.
- The guarantee sounds good but the business cannot fund or fulfill it.
