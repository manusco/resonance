# Visual Style Library

> **Objective**: A vocabulary for aiming a prompt, not a ranking of what is good.

Style is a deliberate choice. Every entry below is tied to a period, a medium, and a mood. None is a default, and none is "premium" on its own. A style earns its place only when it serves the brief. Whether a look reads as timeless or as dated slop is a taste call, and for anything that touches product that call belongs to `resonance-design-designer`. Use these names to describe intent precisely, then let the designer judge.

Two cautions before the list. First, avoid look-of-the-moment surface tricks as a default: heavy frosted-glass blur, decorative asymmetric card mosaics, and the like read as trend markers and age fast. Reach for them only when a brief genuinely calls for that period signal, and clear it with the designer. Second, when you name a style, name it for what the image is, not for a brand or a product; describe the technique and the era, not a logo.

## 1. Photography (marketing, lifestyle, product)

*   **Editorial**: High-fashion framing, sharp focus, controlled studio backdrop, a confident single subject. Reads as considered and expensive when the light is disciplined.
*   **Documentary / candid**: A named film stock, visible grain, natural light, imperfect framing, real emotion. Trades polish for truth.
*   **Product on set**: Clean studio light, deliberate shadow, macro detail on the material. Let the surface and the shadow do the work, not gimmicks.
*   **Cinematic**: A specific color grade stated as intent (for example warm highlights against cool shadow), wide frame, shallow depth. Name the grade rather than a film title.

## 2. Illustration and art

*   **Flat vector**: Solid fills, clean geometry, limited palette. Powerful when the shapes are specific; weak and generic when proportions turn into the interchangeable blob-people look. Use only with a real point of view.
*   **Painterly / watercolor**: Soft pigment washes, layered depth, hand-made edges. Warm and human; slow to read at small sizes.
*   **Pixel art**: A stated bit-depth and era, dithering, a hard palette limit. A period style; use it when the period is the point.
*   **Line art**: Continuous or contour line, minimal fill, ink on paper. Elegant and cheap to reproduce.
*   **Woodblock / print traditions**: Named technique and pigments (for example a woodblock look with mineral blues). Describe the craft, not an artist.

## 3. Dimensional and rendered

*   **Isometric**: Orthographic camera, clean planes, controlled studio light. Reads as tidy and systematic; tips into toy-like fast, so decide which you want.
*   **Soft-3D / molded**: Rounded volumes, matte surfaces, soft light. Friendly and tactile. A dated-trend risk when it turns everything into inflated pastel blobs, so use with intent.
*   **Clay / stop-motion**: Visible fingerprints in the material, real-surface imperfection, practical light. Charming because it looks made by hand.

## 4. Texture and material

*   **Iridescent**: Thin-film color shift, oil-slick spectrum, subtle chromatic edges. High impact, easy to overdo.
*   **Matte**: Non-reflective, soft-touch surfaces (rubber, silicone, uncoated paper). Calm and modern.
*   **Metallic**: A specific metal and finish (brushed aluminum, oxidized copper, gold leaf). State the oxidation and grain.
*   **Organic**: Real-world grain (wood, stone, paper fiber, linen). The most reliable way to make a surface feel true.

## How to use this

Pick the entry that matches the brief, state it as intent in the Style field of the structured prompt, and translate any mood word into a concrete technique (medium, light, era). Never stack three styles hoping one lands; that is how a set loses coherence. When the asset borders on interface or product, run the style past `resonance-design-designer` before you commit.
