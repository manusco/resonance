# Depth and Materials

Depth is one option for expressing grouping, layering, or interaction. Flat, outlined, colored-surface, and shadowed styles can all work. Match the chosen treatment to the product, content, platform, and accessibility needs.

## One light source

If the chosen style uses directional light, keep related shadows and highlights coherent. A flat or outline-based interface does not need a simulated light source.

## Shadow treatments

There is no required shadow recipe. A single shadow, layered shadows, an outline, or no shadow can fit. Tune any chosen shadow to the surface, hierarchy, and contrast needs.

- Multiple layers can help when the surface needs soft ambient depth; use only as many as the chosen style and rendering support.
- Tint can coordinate a shadow with nearby colors. Neutral or black shadows may also fit the palette and contrast needs.
- If the interface uses multiple elevations, make changes distinguishable and consistent with their roles. Distance, blur, and opacity are tuning choices, not a fixed formula.

## Elevation as a system

- If elevation is part of the product's visual language, use a small reusable set where it helps users read layers.
- Map visual separation to interface relationships and interaction. A temporary dialog may need clear separation; not every modal or page needs a shadow.
- In dark themes, test whether shadows, surface contrast, borders, or another cue communicates the needed separation.

## Hairlines and edges

- When an edge helps distinguish regions, choose a separator that remains visible and meets applicable contrast needs. A hairline, solid border, surface change, or gap can fit.
- Highlights can reinforce a raised surface in a directional style; they are unnecessary in a flat or outline-based style.
- Avoid redundant separation cues unless each serves a distinct purpose.

## Texture and grain

- Texture or grain may support a brand or help with a rendering issue, when it does not reduce legibility or task focus.
- Grid and dot patterns can serve data, navigation, or brand expression. Use them when they add meaning; remove them when they are only decoration.

## Glass, only when it earns it

- Backdrop blur can communicate a floating layer over moving content. It can also be decorative or impair legibility in other contexts.
- When used, maintain text contrast and check behavior where blur is unsupported.

## The flat trap and the skeuomorphic trap

- Any visual style must keep controls, surfaces, and interactive states distinguishable. Depth is one possible cue; labels, color, outlines, spacing, and state changes can do that work too.
- Choose flat, material, or skeuomorphic treatments from the brief and platform. Preserve legibility, affordances, and accessible state communication.
