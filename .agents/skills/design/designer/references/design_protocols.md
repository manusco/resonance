# Visual Departures, Deliberately

> A familiar pattern can support speed, while a departure can express identity or make an important difference easier to see. Choose based on the task and product context. A departure may be local, repeated as part of a system, or unnecessary. For the craft that carries the rest of the work, see the first-principles references.

## When breaking expectation earns its place

Brand and product register describe task contexts, not rigid visual classes. Consider whether a familiar pattern helps people recognize and complete the task, or whether a change better expresses the identity or clarifies the content. The same product may need both approaches on different surfaces. State the reason for a departure and check that it preserves orientation and access to important actions.

## How to break it well

- **Choose the scale of a departure.** A focused change can draw attention to one message; a repeated treatment can establish a product language. Use either when it serves the task, and preserve enough shared cues for people to understand where they are and what they can do.
- **Break toward clarity, not away from it.** Massive type that makes the headline unmissable is a good break. A layout so novel the user cannot find the primary action is a failure, not bravery.
- **Earn asymmetry.** Deliberate imbalance (a headline pushed off-center, generous negative space on one side) creates energy and still resolves. Randomness does not. Asymmetry is composed; chaos is not.
- **Question the reflex layout, do not ban it.** A card grid, a hero split, or a bento can be exactly right when the content fits it. The failure is reaching for it by default without asking whether it serves this content. Ask; if it fits, use it.

## What this reference does not own

- **Color.** Do not use crude hex bans here. Perceptual color, contrast hierarchy, and the AI-slop color tells live in color_and_contrast and ai_design_slop.
- **Motion.** Entrance, hover, and press craft live in motion_and_feel.
- **The catalog of machine-made tells** (default sans, purple gradient, gradient text, glass by reflex, identical card grids) lives in ai_design_slop.

The through-line: make visual choices with a reason tied to the people, content, and task. A consistent established pattern and a distinctive custom language can both be well-crafted when they support that work.
