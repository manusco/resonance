# Forward Backlog Protocol (what would make it great next)

> **Objective**: After the audit, write the forward-looking backlog. Not a changelog of what you did. What would make the site great next.
> **Rule**: Rank ruthlessly. Include the bets and the things you would not do yet, and say why.

## FUTURE_IMPROVEMENTS.md (per site)

Write one file at the site root. For each idea, give:

- **The opportunity**: what could be better, in one concrete sentence.
- **Why it matters**: to a real user or to the business. No opportunity without a reason.
- **Effort**: a rough size (hours, days, weeks).
- **Impact**: the expected move, honestly. Name the metric if there is one.
- **Priority**: now, next, later, or not yet.

Group by theme: positioning, conversion, product, content, technical, growth. Rank within each theme, most important first. Keep it in builder voice: concrete nouns, numbers, named files. No slop, no summary of a summary.

Include the tough calls. The bet you are not sure of. The thing you would not do yet, and the reason. A backlog that only lists the safe wins is not honest.

## The done-bar (substantial or nothing)

A page counts as audited-to-standard only when:

- Its job is clear in five seconds, to a first-time visitor, without scrolling.
- There is one unmistakable primary action.
- Every claim has proof next to it.
- Zero console errors, and the page holds on a phone.
- The single most important change for that page is named, and it is in the plan.

If the audit produced only cosmetic moves, it is not done. Go back to the job and the value gap.

## Cross-site master report (multi-site runs only)

When you audit more than one property, write one index at the run root. Per site:

- The health score before.
- The top three forward bets.
- The single most important move.

Then a cross-site section:

- The patterns that repeat: the same copy problem, the same CTA problem, the same missing proof across sites.
- The two or three moves that would lift every site at once.

This is where the impact compounds. One fix pattern applied across six sites beats six one-off tweaks.
