# Page Interrogation Battery (first principles, per page)

> **Objective**: Interrogate one page from first principles. Not a vibe check, a battery. Answer each question with evidence from the actual page, then decide: add, change, rearrange, remove, or leave.
> **Rule**: The current page is evidence, not the target. Derive the answer from the job the page exists to do.

For each dimension, the deep lens belongs to the skill named in **Lens**. This battery adds the framing and the decision. It does not repeat what those skills already teach.

## 1. Purpose and job to be done

**Lens: this skill.** No other skill frames the page's reason to exist.

- What is the single job this page exists to do? State it in one sentence. If you cannot, the page has no focus, and that is finding number one.
- Who is the user standing on this page, and what were they doing one second before they arrived? What is their intent, their fear, their question?
- What is the one action this page should produce? Is everything on the page serving that action, or competing with it?
- What can be removed entirely without hurting the job? Removal is a feature.

## 2. Value: promise and delivery

**Lens: this skill, with /friction for the value-proposition mechanics.**

- What value does the page promise, above the fold, explicitly and implicitly?
- Does it deliver that value, and how fast? Measure time-to-value in seconds and in scroll depth.
- Where is the gap between promise and delivery? That gap is the most important fix on the page.
- Could the same value land in fewer steps, with less thinking, more proof, a sharper demonstration, an interactive instead of a paragraph?

## 3. Copy

**Lens: /copywriter.** Run the humanizer and anti-slop pass. The deep rules live there.

- Read the headline aloud. Is it true and specific, or a placeholder dressed as a headline? Would the target user feel "yes, that is my situation"?
- Is the copy idiomatic in the page's own language, or does it read like a translation or AI filler?
- Does every section earn its place, or is there ritual copy ("Welcome to", "We are passionate about") that says nothing?
- Is there proof next to every claim: a number, a name, a specific, a testimonial, a demo?
- Does the copy speak to the reader's outcome, or describe features from the seller's point of view?

## 4. Clarity and "don't make me think"

**Lens: /design and /friction.**

- Can a first-time visitor understand what this is, who it is for, and what to do next, in five seconds, without scrolling?
- Is there a clear visual hierarchy that leads the eye to the one action, or does everything shout at once? Run the squint test.
- Is any label, icon, or step ambiguous? Would a non-expert know what happens when they click?
- Count the decisions the page forces on the user. Cut the ones that do not need to exist.

## 5. Call to action and conversion

**Lens: /friction.** The Friction Collider and the LIFT audit live there.

- Is there one primary CTA, unmistakable, above the fold and repeated at the natural decision points?
- Does the CTA label state the outcome ("Get my valuation"), not the mechanism ("Submit")?
- What friction sits between intent and action: too many fields, an unclear next step, no trust signal at the moment of commitment, a dead end?
- Is there an honest reason to act now?
- For every form or tool: what happens on empty, on error, on a slow network, on mobile, on hostile input? Walk the unhappy paths.

## 6. Design and craft

**Lens: /design.** The craft, the states, and the slop catalog live there.

- Does the page look like the top 1 percent built it, or like a template? Hierarchy, spacing, rhythm, perceptual color, type scale.
- Does the layout hold at 375px, 768px, and 1280px, and on a slow connection?
- Are hover, focus, loading, empty, and error states designed, or default? Is anything janky?
- Is the detail layer right: favicons, OG images, focus rings, alt text, consistent icons?

## 7. Function and correctness

**Lens: /audit and /test.**

- Does every interactive element actually work? Click it, submit it, break it on purpose.
- Console errors, network failures, broken links, 404s, dead endpoints, forms that go nowhere: find them and kill them.
- For any calculator, configurator, or tool: is the core feature correct against its own methodology doc? Verify the math and the flow end to end.

## 8. Performance, SEO, accessibility, trust

**Lens: /seo and /audit.**

- Core Web Vitals: LCP, CLS, INP. Bundle weight, image formats and sizes, fonts. What is slow and why.
- SEO and GEO: title, meta, headings, structured data, sitemap, robots, canonical, OG. Is the page citable by an AI and findable by a human?
- Accessibility: keyboard path, focus order, contrast, semantic landmarks, labels, reduced motion. WCAG as a floor.
- Trust: is it obvious who is behind this, is contact real, is the legal footing right (Impressum, privacy, cookie handling for German sites), do trust signals appear at the moment of doubt?

## 9. Non-web surfaces

The frame generalizes. For an app or a game, translate the terms:

- "Pages" become screens and states: menu, onboarding, the core loop, results, settings.
- "Value promise vs delivery" becomes the core experience landing fast: does the main mechanic or the aha moment arrive before the user gives up?
- "Copy" becomes onboarding and in-context text.
- "CTA and conversion" becomes the path from first open to first completed session, and the clarity of the next move at every state.

Audit each state the way you would audit a page: what is its job, does it deliver, is the next move obvious.
