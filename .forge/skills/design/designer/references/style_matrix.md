# Visual Direction Axes

Use these prompts when a brief calls for visual exploration or a new product system. They help turn the audience, content, task, platform, and existing identity into a coherent direction. They are not presets or required deliverables. Use one direction, combine compatible qualities, or stay with the established system when that serves the work.

- **Typographic voice:** What should type communicate, and what must readers scan or compare? Select typefaces, weights, scale, and line lengths for the content, brand, script coverage, and legibility needs.
- **Palette roles:** Which surfaces, text, emphasis, and status roles need distinct meaning? Choose hue, saturation, temperature, and contrast from the brief and applicable accessibility targets. Achromatic, tinted, vivid, muted, pure black, and pure white palettes can all fit.
- **Shape and edges:** Should the interface feel precise, soft, expressive, or restrained? Choose geometry and corner relationships that fit the product. Related controls can share a language while purposeful exceptions signal hierarchy or function.
- **Density and composition:** How much information must be visible together? Choose spacing, grouping, alignment, and page composition around the task, content volume, and responsive behavior.
- **Surface and depth:** Does the product benefit from flat color, outlines, tonal surfaces, texture, or elevation? Use only treatments that clarify hierarchy or identity, and make repeated treatments coherent.

When offering directions, describe the choices and their fit instead of assigning a preset label. Keep examples visibly illustrative, not as palette, type, radius, or layout requirements. The product's own system should remain coherent within its roles; coherence does not require every project to share the same visual language.
