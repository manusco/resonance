# Deliberation Protocol

Use this protocol to keep a council analytical rather than theatrical.

## Decision packet

Give every specialist the same bounded packet:

- artifact and decision to review
- intended outcome and success criteria
- verified facts and sources
- directives, constraints, and non-goals
- assumptions and missing evidence
- stakes, reversibility, and deadline

Label facts, user directives, and inferences separately. Do not fill missing context with invented certainty. `/brief` owns general intent framing. Council framing only makes the reviewable decision explicit.

## Specialist selection

Choose three specialists by default and no more than five. Select mandates because they can materially change the recommendation. Avoid decorative personas and fixed casts. State each mandate, its likely blind spot, and why it belongs.

## Blind openings and rebuttal

Each specialist writes an opening position before seeing the others. Require: verdict, evidence, assumptions, strongest objection, failure case, and what would change the view.

Run one rebuttal round. Each specialist challenges the weakest material assumption in another position and the strongest objection to its own. Do not add rounds unless the user explicitly requests deeper analysis.

## Reconciliation

The chair reconciles evidence, not votes. Preserve:

- agreements and their evidential basis
- disagreements and the facts or values causing them
- minority objections with high potential harm
- scenarios that change the preferred option
- recommendation, confidence, and reversal conditions

Same-model subagents provide separate context passes, not independent expert evidence. Name their provenance. If true independence matters, route one concrete artifact to `/second-opinion` once, subject to confidentiality and approval boundaries.

## Safety and authority

Never auto-execute the recommendation. Stop for the user at consequential or one-way decisions. Do not disclose sensitive material to external reviewers without explicit approval. Prefer local analysis, redaction, or a bounded summary.
