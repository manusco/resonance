# Support Triage and Deflection

> Support and customer success are different jobs. Support is reactive and resolves incidents; success is proactive and drives outcomes. Triage and deflect the repeatable so the human stays on the proactive work that actually moves retention. And read the ticket stream: it is a live health and product signal.

## 1. Support is not customer success

Two jobs, often confused, measured on different numbers.

| | Support | Customer Success |
|---|---------|------------------|
| **Posture** | Reactive: a problem arrives | Proactive: drive the outcome |
| **Unit** | The ticket | The relationship and the outcome |
| **Question** | How do I do X | Are you getting the value you bought, and what is next |
| **Measured on** | Resolution time, CSAT | NRR, renewal, health |

Collapse success into a support queue and the CSM becomes a ticket-closer who drives no outcomes and moves no retention. The two functions feed each other, but a founder must protect the proactive time, because it is the first thing an incoming ticket eats.

## 2. Triage by severity

Not every ticket deserves the same speed or the same owner. Route by impact.

| Severity | Definition | Response |
|----------|------------|----------|
| **Critical** | Blocked, down, revenue or data at risk | Immediate, human, all hands if needed |
| **High** | A core workflow broken, a real business impact | Fast, human, tracked to resolution |
| **Standard** | A question or a minor issue with a workaround | Queue, deflect where possible |
| **Low** | How-to, cosmetic, feature wish | Deflect to self-serve, log the wish |

Triage protects both the customer and the team: the critical issue gets the human, and the how-to gets the doc, so neither starves the other.

## 3. Deflect the repeatable, escalate the novel

Deflection is not about answering fewer questions. It is about answering the repeatable ones without a human, so the human is free for the ones that need judgment.

- **Docs and search** for the questions asked the same way every time. A good help center is the cheapest support you will ever run.
- **In-app help** at the point of confusion beats a doc the customer has to leave the product to find.
- **Community** where users answer each other, which also builds stickiness.
- **Escalate the novel.** A question no doc answers is a signal: either a gap in the product, a gap in onboarding, or a real edge case that deserves a human. Deflect the known, escalate the new, and turn each escalation into next month's deflected doc.

The goal is not zero human contact. It is human contact spent where judgment is needed, not on the same password-reset answer for the hundredth time.

## 4. Tickets as a health signal

The ticket stream is a live feed into account health, and it belongs in the health model.

- **A ticket spike on one account is a health event.** Rising volume, or a sharp change in tone, is an early churn signal. Route it to the CSM, not just the support queue.
- **Silence can also be a signal.** An account that went from active to no contact may be disengaging, not content.
- **Escalation history** is one of the sentiment inputs to the health score. A string of frustrated tickets outweighs a survey answer.

Wire support and success together so a support pattern reaches the person who owns the relationship before the renewal, not after.

## 5. Tickets as a product signal

Aggregated tickets are voice-of-customer data with a volume attached, which makes them easy to weight.

- **Cluster the repeatable.** The same question asked a thousand times is either a documentation gap or a product gap. High-volume how-to tickets often point at a confusing design, not a teaching problem.
- **Weight by frequency and revenue.** A pain felt by many accounts, or by a few large ones, ranks above a loud one-off.
- **Hand product the ranked list, not anecdotes.** You supply weighted signal; product owns the roadmap decision. A single dramatic ticket is a story; a weighted cluster is evidence.

## 6. Anti-patterns

- **CSM as the support queue.** The proactive role drowned in reactive tickets, so no outcome work happens and retention drifts.
- **Deflection as a wall.** Hiding the human behind a bot maze so the critical issue cannot reach a person. Deflect the repeatable, never the urgent.
- **Tickets in a silo.** Support data that never reaches the CSM or product, so the churn signal and the roadmap signal both die in the helpdesk.
- **Vanity deflection.** Optimizing deflection rate while CSAT falls, which just means customers gave up, not that they were helped.

## 7. Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Read it with |
|--------|------------|--------------|
| Deflection rate | Issues resolved self-serve / total contacts | CSAT, so you deflect by helping, not by hiding |
| Time to resolution | Speed to close, by severity | Severity mix, so criticals are not buried in the average |
| CSAT | Satisfaction on resolved contacts | The check that deflection did not just frustrate |
| Tickets per account trend | Volume direction per account | The health signal; a spike is a churn warning |

Deflection rate on its own is a vanity number. Read it against CSAT, because a customer who gave up looks identical to one who was helped, until they churn.
